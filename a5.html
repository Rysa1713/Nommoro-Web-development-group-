Packaging that Preserves

-July 12th 2025

What are the types of packaging?

Choosing the right packaging plays a big role in keeping food fresh, safe, and flavorful. According to PWR Pack, different food items require different types of packaging like plastic films for moisture control, glass jars for airtight seals, and metals for durability and preservation. Understanding how these materials work helps consumers make better choices at home too. PWR

Reuse Glass Jars for Airtight Storage: Glass jars from pasta sauces or jams are great for storing grains, nuts, sauces, or leftovers. They don’t absorb odors and create a strong oxygen and moisture barrier — similar to commercial canning.

Make Your Own Beeswax Wraps: Instead of cling film, you can DIY food wraps using cotton cloth, melted beeswax, and coconut oil. These wraps are flexible, reusable, and perfect for covering bowls, cheese, fruits, or sandwiches.

Vacuum Seal Without a Machine: Place food in a zip-lock bag and submerge it slowly in water to force air out before sealing. This method helps reduce oxidation and keeps meats or leftovers fresher in the freezer.

Sew Breathable Cotton Bags for Produce: Old T-shirts or thin cloth can be transformed into drawstring produce bags. These are ideal for storing fruits and vegetables that need some air circulation, like onions or apples.

Add a DIY Moisture Absorber: For dry food storage (like crackers or herbs), include a small food-safe silica gel pack or some dry rice wrapped in tissue to absorb moisture and prevent sogginess.

💡 Pro Tip: Label and Date Everything

Even the best DIY packaging won't help if you forget what's inside. Use masking tape or reusable labels to mark the contents and date on every jar, wrap, or bag. This simple habit helps prevent waste, keeps your storage organized, and lets you track how well each packaging method preserves your food over time.
