-July 12th 2025

Don't Throw Away Good Food - Know Your Date Labels!

Millions of tonnes of food are wasted every year, often because people don't understand what date labels really mean. The two main types of date labels in the UK are 'best before' and 'use by' dates, and knowing the difference can help you save money and reduce food waste.

Most food is safe to eat after the 'best before' date, but may not be at its best quality. However, food should not be eaten after its 'use by' date as this could put your health at risk.

🏷️ Understanding Date Labels: Best Before vs Use By

Best Before: This is about quality, not safety. Food is still safe to eat after this date, but may not be at its best quality. Use your judgment - if it looks, smells, and tastes fine, it's likely okay.

Use By: This is about safety. Don't eat food after this date as it could be unsafe and cause food poisoning, even if it looks and smells fine.

Display Until/Sell By: These are instructions for shop staff, not for home use. You can still eat the food after this date if it's within the 'use by' or 'best before' date.

Smart Tips to Reduce Food Waste

Check before you chuck: Look, smell, and taste (if appropriate) before throwing food away. Many foods past their 'best before' date are perfectly fine to eat.

Plan your meals: Buy only what you need and use food in date order - older items first.

Store food properly: Keep your fridge at 5°C or below and follow storage instructions to help food last longer and stay safe.

Freeze before the date: You can freeze most foods before their 'use by' date. Remember to defrost thoroughly and use within 24 hours of defrosting.

For more detailed guidance, visit the UK Food Standards Agency
