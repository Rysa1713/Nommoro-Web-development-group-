Go Green!

-July 12th 2025

How Long Can You Keep Leftovers?

Food waste doesn’t have to end up in the bin. With home composting, you can turn everyday scraps into nutrient-rich soil that benefits your garden, saves money, and helps the environment. Composting is nature’s way of recycling—and it’s easier than most people think. Whether you have a backyard or live in an apartment, there’s a composting method that can work for your lifestyle.

Composting reduces the amount of food waste sent to landfills, where it would otherwise produce methane—a potent greenhouse gas. It also creates natural fertilizer that improves soil health, retains moisture, and supports strong plant growth. By composting at home, you’re not only cutting waste but contributing to a more sustainable planet right from your kitchen.
Read More Here: EPA

💡 Pro Tip: Start Small with Indoor Composting

New to composting? Begin with a small countertop compost bin for your kitchen scraps! Even apartment dwellers can reduce waste by collecting fruit peels, coffee grounds, and eggshells. If you don't have outdoor space, consider bokashi composting or worm composting—both work great indoors and produce nutrient-rich soil for your houseplants.

Easy Home Composting Guide: What & How

Food Scraps Composting Method & Tips
Fruit & Vegetable Peels Chop into small pieces for faster decomposition. Perfect for any compost method.
Coffee Grounds & Tea Bags Remove staples from tea bags. Mix coffee grounds with "brown" materials like dry leaves.
Eggshells Crush into small pieces. Add calcium to your compost and help balance pH levels.
Bread & Grains (small amounts) Bury deep in compost pile to avoid attracting pests. Best in hot composting systems.
Paper Napkins & Towels Unbleached only. Tear into pieces and mix with food scraps for balanced composting.
Yard Trimmings Layer grass clippings with dry leaves. Avoid diseased plants and weeds with seeds.
Hair & Pet Fur Natural and nutrient-rich! Add in small amounts to any composting system.
